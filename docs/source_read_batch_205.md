# Source-read clinical extraction batch 205

Three additional eligible reports were source-checked and converted into structured clinical result rows.

## Arruda et al. 2025 — female factory workers in Southern Brazil (FC143-00761)

Two repeated cross-sectional samples of 400 women were assessed in 2017 and 2022. Among workers with occupational stress, night-shift work was associated with abdominal obesity relative to day work:
- 2017: adjusted PR 2.23 (95% CI 1.47-3.38; p<0.001);
- 2022: adjusted PR 1.80 (1.06-3.06; p=0.029).

No significant shift-abdominal-obesity association was observed among workers without occupational stress. These are effect-modified cross-sectional associations, not longitudinal effects.

## Zhang et al. 2021 — Wenzhou tertiary-hospital staff (FC143-02046)

Among 1,176 doctors and nurses, adjusted occupational-stress models showed lower HDL cholesterol with:
- occupational role stress (ORQ): standardized beta -0.497 (95% CI -0.511 to -0.322; p<0.001);
- personal strain (PSQ): beta -0.338 (-0.463 to -0.029; p<0.001).

HDL is a prespecified MetS component. The study is cross-sectional and the two coefficients arise from the same sample.

## Manaf et al. 2021 — Malaysian public-university staff (FC143-00632)

Among 538 university staff, overall MetS prevalence was 20.6%. In the age-adjusted multivariable model, moderate-intensity physical activity was associated with higher odds of MetS (aOR 2.5, 95% CI 1.2-5.0; p=0.015). The direction is counterintuitive and should not be interpreted causally; self-reported activity, reverse causation and residual confounding remain plausible.

# Final Outcome Rule for Path A

## Inclusion rule

A workplace study is eligible if it reports either:

1. **metabolic syndrome as a defined syndrome** (prevalence, incidence, severity, diagnosis, remission, or risk); or
2. **one or more recognized metabolic-syndrome components** that are explicitly framed as part of MetS prevention, risk, severity, or management.

Recognized components include:
- central/abdominal obesity or waist circumference;
- elevated blood pressure;
- impaired fasting glucose/hyperglycaemia;
- elevated triglycerides;
- reduced HDL cholesterol.

## Exclusion rule

Studies are excluded if they report only:
- generic stress;
- physical activity;
- work ability;
- liver enzymes;
- general cardiovascular fitness;
- broad cardiometabolic biomarkers

without a clear MetS or MetS-component framing.

## Why this rule

This preserves the review's focus on metabolic syndrome while allowing workplace prevention/intervention studies that target individual MetS components before a full syndrome diagnosis develops.

## Consequences

- Lindberg et al. (2018) remains excluded because it focuses on office workstation type, physical activity, and stress without MetS outcomes.
- The 2026 Ghana university-worker study remains **eligible only at the component level** because it evaluates abdominal obesity, blood pressure, glucose/lipid-related markers in an explicitly cardiometabolic/MetS-relevant framework.
- The 2026 Ghana healthcare-professional and military studies are clearly eligible because MetS was explicitly defined and reported.

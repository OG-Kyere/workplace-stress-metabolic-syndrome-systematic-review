# Risk-of-Bias and Critical Appraisal Framework

## Decision

The revised systematic review will use **JBI design-specific critical appraisal tools** across the mixed study designs.

This approach provides one coherent appraisal framework while respecting differences between randomized, longitudinal observational, cross-sectional, and nonrandomized intervention studies.

## Tool assignment

### Randomized controlled trials
Use the **JBI Critical Appraisal Tool for Randomized Controlled Trials**.

Current candidate RCTs:
- Haufe et al. (2019)
- Ma et al. (2025)
- Bogale et al. (2025)

### Cohort / longitudinal observational studies
Use the **JBI Critical Appraisal Tool for Cohort Studies**.

Current candidates:
- Garbarino & Magnavita (2015)
- Kuwahara et al. (2016)
- Yamaguchi et al. (2018)
- van Zon et al. (2020)
- Runge et al. (2021)

### Analytical cross-sectional studies
Use the **JBI Assessment of Risk of Bias for Analytical Cross-sectional Studies**.

This is the largest design group in the review and includes the occupational prevalence/association studies.

### Quasi-experimental / nonrandomized intervention studies
Use the **JBI Critical Appraisal Tool for Quasi-Experimental Studies**.

Current candidates:
- Earnest & Church (2015)
- Kim et al. (2016)
- Ryu et al. (2017)
- So et al. (2025)

## Appraisal procedure

1. Read the complete report for each included study.
2. Appraise each study using the checklist assigned to its design.
3. Record each item as **Yes / No / Unclear / Not applicable** where permitted by the tool.
4. Add a short justification for every non-Yes judgment.
5. Have at least two reviewers appraise independently where feasible.
6. Resolve disagreements through discussion; use a third reviewer if consensus cannot be reached.
7. Report domain/item judgments transparently rather than relying only on a summed score.

## No arbitrary quality cut-off

Studies will **not** be excluded solely because they receive a low numerical checklist total.

Risk-of-bias findings will instead be used to:
- qualify the narrative synthesis;
- identify which findings are more or less trustworthy;
- support sensitivity analyses if quantitative synthesis becomes appropriate;
- explain limitations in the Discussion.

## Important principle

Eligibility and methodological quality are separate.

A study can meet the review's inclusion criteria while still having serious risk-of-bias concerns. The appraisal stage evaluates credibility; it does not retroactively redefine eligibility.

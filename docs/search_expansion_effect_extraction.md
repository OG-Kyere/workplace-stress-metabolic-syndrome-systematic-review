# Search Expansion During Effect Extraction

Effect-estimate extraction and reference chasing identified six additional primary workplace studies that meet the Path A scope and publication-date rule.

## Newly identified studies

1. **Kim et al. (2015)** — Internet-based lifestyle intervention among Korean workers with metabolic syndrome.
   - Controlled clinical trial.
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.pec.2014.10.013
   - Published 2015.

2. **Choi et al. (2017)** — T'ai Chi-based worksite health-promotion program in middle-aged male office workers at high risk of metabolic syndrome.
   - Randomized trial.
   - DOI: 10.1089/acm.2017.0057

3. **Lin et al. (2017)** — Relationships between occupational, leisure-time and commuting physical activity and metabolic syndrome among Taiwanese workers.
   - Analytical cross-sectional study.
   - PMID: 29061986

4. **Tsai et al. (2018)** — Physical activity program among overweight employees in high-tech industries.
   - Randomized controlled study.
   - PMID: 29465293

5. **Gerber et al. (2020)** — Stress-buffering effects of physical activity and cardiorespiratory fitness on metabolic syndrome in police officers.
   - Prospective study.
   - PMID: 32722703

6. **Remote physical-activity trial (2024)** — Remote physical activity improvement program among male office workers with metabolic syndrome and sedentary behaviour.
   - Randomized controlled trial.
   - PMID: 38286191

## Consequence

The actively screened primary-study pool increases from **28 to 34 studies**.

This reinforces an important methodological point: the fresh search must remain open until the formal database search, reference-list screening, and deduplication are completed. The number 34 is a current working inclusion pool, not yet the final PRISMA inclusion count.

## Related records not added as independent studies

- Haufe maintenance/wearable-device follow-up papers derive from the same Volkswagen intervention programme and should be treated as companion reports unless they answer a separate prespecified review question.
- The Garbarino police sleep-analysis paper derives from the same rapid-response police cohort and focuses primarily on sleep mediation; it should be treated as a companion report rather than a new independent cohort.
- The 2021 systematic review/meta-analysis of workplace diet/lifestyle interventions is a secondary source and is useful for reference mining, not primary-study inclusion.

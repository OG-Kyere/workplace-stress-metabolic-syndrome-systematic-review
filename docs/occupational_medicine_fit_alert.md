# Target-journal fit alert — Occupational Medicine

Checked against the current Oxford Academic author guidelines on 6 October 2026.

## Important finding

The current `Occupational Medicine` article-type guidance contains an unusual distinction.

Under **Systematic Reviews**, the journal states that the review should:
- discuss and critically evaluate developments in the field;
- guide practising occupational-health professionals through the literature;
- include a paragraph describing the literature-search method;
- be **qualitative rather than systematic in style**;
- differ from a PRISMA-based systematic review.

The same section sets:
- structured abstract: 250 words;
- teaser: 75 words;
- references: maximum 70;
- tables/figures: maximum 6;
- main text: maximum 5000 words.

## Implication for the current manuscript

The current manuscript is a genuine systematic-review workflow:
- explicit eligibility criteria;
- fresh search and re-screening;
- study-level extraction;
- design-specific JBI appraisal;
- planned PRISMA 2020 flow;
- record-level screening reconstruction;
- structured evidence synthesis.

Therefore, the manuscript currently fits **PRISMA-style systematic-review reporting more closely than the qualitative review style described by Occupational Medicine**.

## Risk

Submitting the manuscript unchanged to Occupational Medicine may create an article-type mismatch at editorial screening.

The paper could still be scientifically relevant to the journal, but the current instructions make the fit uncertain because the manuscript is more methodologically systematic than the review format the journal describes.

## Recommended handling

Do **not** dismantle the current PRISMA-oriented manuscript to force it into the journal's qualitative review format yet.

Instead:

1. preserve the current non-PRISMA freeze;
2. treat Occupational Medicine as a **conditional target** rather than a locked target;
3. complete the PRISMA reconstruction because it strengthens the paper regardless of target;
4. compare alternative occupational/public-health journals that explicitly accept PRISMA systematic reviews and permit a no-APC subscription/publication route;
5. only retarget formatting after the journal decision is made.

## Current status

The manuscript remains frozen in its evidence and synthesis content.

No substantive manuscript edits were made in response to this alert.

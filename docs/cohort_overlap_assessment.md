# Cohort-Overlap Assessment

## Dutch Lifelines reports

Two eligible papers use the Lifelines Cohort Study and Biobank:

### van Zon et al. (2020)
- Approximately 75,000 Dutch workers.
- Examines occupational distribution of MetS prevalence and incidence.
- Compares 40 occupational groups.
- Evaluates whether age and health behaviours explain occupational differences.

### Runge et al. (2021)
- 34,834 workers aged 45–65 years.
- Uses two comprehensive Lifelines measurement waves.
- Focuses on **incident MetS among older workers** and whether health behaviours explain occupational differences.

## Decision

**Retain both reports**, because they address distinct analytic questions and populations/time structures.

However, they should be treated as **two reports from one underlying cohort** during narrative synthesis.

### Synthesis rule

- Do not describe them as independent population samples when summarising geographical or cohort counts.
- Avoid double-counting participants in any aggregate sample-size statement.
- Discuss their findings together under a Lifelines/Dutch workforce subsection where appropriate.
- If a meta-analysis were ever attempted, overlapping estimates must not be entered as statistically independent observations without additional handling.

## Other possible overlap

The three 2026 Ghana studies by Ayeltigah and colleagues involve:
- healthcare professionals;
- university workers;
- military personnel.

Current records indicate distinct occupational populations and settings, so they are treated as separate studies unless full-text participant details show otherwise.

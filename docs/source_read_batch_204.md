# Source-read clinical extraction batch 204

Three additional eligible reports were checked against original publication sources and converted into structured result rows.

## Tefera et al. 2022 — Ethiopian government office workers (FC143-00381)

Among 373 workers, central obesity prevalence was 54.7% (95% CI 50.1%-59.8%). Multivariable logistic models reported:
- workplace sitting >=5 h/day: AOR 2.911 (95% CI 1.644-5.156);
- physical inactivity: AOR 4.594 (2.313-9.125);
- snack consumption: AOR 4.521 (2.583-7.913).

These are cross-sectional associations and are not interpreted causally.

## Theodoro et al. 2021 — Brazilian female shift workers (FC143-01515)

Among 450 female shift workers, abdominal obesity prevalence was 44.5%. In adjusted robust-Poisson models:
- night versus hybrid shift: PR 1.33 (95% CI 1.08-1.64; p=0.008);
- leisure-time physical activity: PR 0.71 (0.54-0.95; p=0.02);
- <=3 versus >3 meals/day: PR 1.46 (1.17-1.82; p=0.001).

The outcome was abdominal obesity defined by waist circumference >=88 cm.

## Magnusson Hanson et al. 2017 — CONSTANCES workers (FC143-01606)

The study included 43,593 working adults. With age and socioeconomic adjustment, work stress was associated with higher waist circumference in men (standardized beta 0.08, p<0.001) and women (beta 0.06, p<0.001), and lower HDL cholesterol in men (beta -0.06, p<0.001). The source states that the principal adiposity and HDL associations persisted after broader adjustment, but exact fully adjusted coefficients are not substituted where they are not directly available in accessible text.

# Workplace stress, dietary factors, physical activity and metabolic syndrome among working adults: a systematic review

Working manuscript, 17 October 2026. Selection counts are provisional. Study-level synthesis remains in development; the historical 34-report draft does not represent the current evidence set.

## Introduction

This review examines metabolic syndrome and its recognized components in employed adults. Its focus is the relationship between occupational stress, dietary factors, physical activity, sedentary behaviour and metabolic outcomes, together with workplace interventions addressing these exposures. The workplace context is central to eligibility: a general adult or clinical population is insufficient without an occupational population or a relevant worker-specific analysis.

The review distinguishes a diagnosis of metabolic syndrome from its individual components. It also distinguishes associations observed in occupational populations from effects estimated in intervention studies. These distinctions guide the extraction and synthesis, avoiding substitution of a broader cardiovascular marker for an eligible metabolic outcome or interpretation of a cross-sectional association as an intervention effect.

The objective is to synthesize eligible empirical evidence published from 2015 to the documented search cutoff in 2026, identifying the populations, exposures, outcomes and methodological limitations that determine how each finding can be interpreted.

## Methods

### Review history and eligibility

An archived undergraduate thesis provided the starting reference set. Its original screening spreadsheet was unavailable. The updated review therefore uses a reconstructed search and screening record rather than presenting the recovered records as the original historical database export. The archived thesis and historical study table are retained separately from the current evidence registers.

Eligibility requires an employed adult or explicitly occupational population; occupational stress, dietary factors, physical activity, sedentary behaviour or a relevant workplace lifestyle exposure/intervention; an eligible metabolic outcome; and completed original empirical research in an English-language peer-reviewed report within the publication window. The operational outcome rule accepts defined metabolic syndrome or recognized components explicitly related to metabolic-syndrome risk, prevention, severity or management. Components include central adiposity, blood pressure, fasting glycaemia, triglycerides and HDL cholesterol. BMI, fitness, stress scores or unrelated biomarkers alone do not establish outcome eligibility. Protocols, editorials, conference abstracts, secondary reviews and studies without an occupational context are excluded from the primary evidence synthesis.

Study design is determined from the reported methods. The broad criterion of completed original empirical research does not impose an undocumented categorical exclusion of every genetic design. Scope interpretations requiring confirmation are recorded explicitly in the screening evidence.

### Search and record management

The reconstructed PubMed/MEDLINE core query combines metabolic-syndrome terms with occupational population terms and stress, activity, sedentary, diet or nutrition terms, with publication limits covering 2015–2026. The executed query responses and downloaded records are preserved in the search archive. Additional source occurrences derive from documented supplementary queries, recovered references, review-reference mining, citation searching and version/companion discovery. A query's intended publication window is distinct from the date on which it was executed. No claim is made that publications appearing after a documented search execution were prospectively covered by that search.

Source occurrences are retained separately from canonical deduplicated records. Identifiers, bibliographic metadata and version relationships support reconciliation. Corrections, protocols and related publications are assessed according to their actual report type and contribution rather than assumed to be independent studies. Exact supplementary-search coverage and the final search cutoff require completion of the search-method reconciliation before the flow counts are frozen.

### Selection and unavailable reports

Selection proceeds through title/abstract screening and full-text assessment. Controlled exclusion reasons and the supporting evidence are recorded at the relevant stage. Late title/abstract exclusions remain distinguishable from full-text exclusions even when retrieval had already been attempted.

Existing retrieval attempts are retained. Further repeated retrieval attempts are deferred so that extraction and writing can proceed from available evidence. A report not retrieved is recorded separately from a retrieved report excluded for failing eligibility. A provisional record lacking archived original bytes is not automatically reclassified as a report never retrieved: previous web-based inspection, original-source verification and retention of a local copy are distinct states. Where eligibility cannot be established, it remains unresolved. A secondary review's account is not substituted for independently verified original numerical results.

Screening decisions and appraisal records currently constitute a Codex-assisted provisional first review. Independent reviewer verification has not been completed. This manuscript does not describe the process as independent duplicate screening.

### Data extraction and appraisal

Extraction records occupational population, design, recruitment and analysis denominators, exposure coding, comparator, metabolic outcome definition, follow-up, model adjustment, reported effect and uncertainty, and source location. Between-group intervention contrasts are distinguished from within-group change. Different adjustment models, outcome definitions, time points and participant strata remain separate. Null findings are retained; statistical significance is not an eligibility requirement.

Existing extraction fields are consolidated with their original file, row and hash provenance. Raw table cells support source review but do not by themselves constitute verified clinical effect estimates. Points outside their printed intervals and other internal source inconsistencies are retained as explicit quantitative holds; corrected values are not inferred. Lack of a numeric estimate in the current ledgers is not itself a reason to exclude an otherwise eligible report.

Design-specific JBI item assessments are recorded as Yes, No, Unclear or Not applicable where permitted. Current answers are provisional source-section assessments. They do not establish that full-report appraisal or independent checking is complete. Unclear answers remain visible rather than being replaced by assumed favourable judgments.

### Companion reports and synthesis

Reports are linked to provisional study-family identifiers when a common cohort or trial is identified. These identifiers do not yet certify complete participant independence. Population totals and pooled estimates will require reconciliation of recruitment periods, settings, eligibility, participant overlap and distinct outcomes across companion papers.

The synthesis will distinguish occupational stress, diet/nutrition, physical activity/sedentary behaviour and multicomponent workplace interventions. Work schedules and other related exposures will be described according to their operational eligibility classification. Observational associations will be separated from intervention effects, and syndrome-level outcomes from individual components. No pooled estimate or direction-of-effect conclusion is asserted in this working draft before study-level extraction and comparability assessment are complete.

## Results

### Selection: current provisional record

The current reconstruction contains 9,074 source occurrences. Removal of 3,725 duplicates leaves 5,349 canonical records screened, with 4,381 title/abstract exclusions and 968 reports sought for retrieval. Of those reports, 166 are recorded as not retrieved and 802 as available for full-text assessment. Available reports comprise 413 exclusions, 388 provisional inclusions and one report awaiting an age-eligibility decision. The 388 retained reports map to 372 provisional family identifiers; these are not presented as 372 confirmed independent cohorts or trials.

The current evidence, supplement and reference registers agree on the 388 provisional report identifiers. The older 34-report manuscript table and bibliography are historical artifacts and do not establish the current final study count. Final inclusion and flow totals remain subject to eligibility and search-method reconciliation.

### Availability and extraction

Among the 388 provisionally included reports, 343 have archived original article files, two have primary web-source review captures without verified original bytes, and 43 rely on review evidence pending original-source reconciliation. These 45 original-source gaps are distinct from the 166 reports recorded as not retrieved in the selection flow; the categories must not be added together or treated as interchangeable.

The 14 audited numerical/outcome ledgers contain 1,451 rows for 116 current reports, with exact source files and hashes verified for those rows. The remaining 272 reports have no rows in those particular ledgers, which does not establish absence of prior extraction elsewhere. Consolidation recovered prior structured fields for 71 of these 272 reports. Their next extraction actions involve 160 available articles, 67 PDF originals needing manual structured capture, and 45 original-source gaps.

The numerical audit identified 25 diagnostic flags: 17 point estimates outside printed intervals, seven CI/p-value compatibility warnings and one p-value outside the range zero to one. These are flags, not 25 independent erroneous studies. Model differences, rounding, table alignment and original reporting must be checked before a correction is accepted. Six missing carried-down exposure labels were restored in the processed audit from the original table, resolving two apparent conflicts between results for different exposures. Original numbers and historical ledgers were preserved.

### Appraisal and substantive findings

Provisional design-specific item answers cover all 388 retained reports, comprising 3,516 answers with 1,927 recorded as Unclear. Independent appraisal reviews completed: zero. These counts describe the current assessment record and do not support a conclusion that the evidence is generally high or low quality.

The following source-checked findings are an initial subset, not a synthesis of all 388 retained reports. The previous 34-report narrative is not carried forward as a conclusion about the expanded set. No pooled effect, aggregate participant total or claim that one exposure domain has the most consistent evidence is made at this stage. The current specified-results pass adds 43 rows from six original articles, bringing coverage across the 14 audited ledgers and this new ledger to 122 reports; 266 have no rows in those sources. A report with some extracted rows is not necessarily fully extracted or independently verified.

### Workplace lifestyle education: between-group findings

In the Ethiopian bank-worker trial, 226 participants were allocated and 207 retained for analysis (105 intervention, 102 control). The reported adjusted GEE intervention-by-time coefficients were −1.501 cm for waist circumference (95% CI −4.052 to 1.049), −2.453 mmHg for systolic pressure (−6.289 to 1.382), and −0.620 mmHg for diastolic pressure (−3.181 to 1.941). All three intervals included zero. Triglyceride and fasting-glucose interaction coefficients also had intervals including zero: −16.281 mg/dL (−38.565 to 6.003) and −1.714 mg/dL (−9.417 to 5.990), respectively. HDL cholesterol decreased relative to the control time trend, with a coefficient of −5.055 mg/dL (−7.805 to −2.306); a decrease in HDL is not a favourable component change. These estimates differ from the larger reductions observed within the intervention arm. Allocation involved only two geographical clusters, one per arm, and recruitment followed allocation; individual-level GEE results do not resolve uncertainty about cluster-level treatment inference. Loss-to-follow-up exclusions also require reconciliation with the stated intention-to-treat analysis. [Bogale et al., 2025, Table 4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-22962-8).

### Physical activity and sedentary exposure

Among 344 employees of a Lebanese university, the original table reported MetS in 54 of 221 employees with low IPAQ activity and 27 of 123 with moderate/high activity. The unadjusted categorical comparison gave p=0.698. The paper therefore contributes an eligible activity comparison without clear evidence of an association in that analysis; its vitamin-D-status regression is not substituted for a dietary-intake or adjusted activity effect. [Ghadieh et al., 2018, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40795-018-0243-x).

In 373 currently employed Buffalo police officers, the mean number of MetS components across increasing physical-activity-intensity categories was 1.78, 1.57, 1.80 and 1.43, with respective SDs of 1.50, 1.37, 1.42 and 1.46 and a reported trend p=0.2112. This secondary, unadjusted comparison is distinct from the paper's main cortisol analysis. The protein-meal challenge was a physiological test and is not interpreted as a dietary-treatment effect. Activity-intensity units require clarification, and the BCOPS cohort relationship must be reconciled before combining this paper with companion reports. [Baughman et al., 2016, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajhb.22748).

A Korean survey analysis reported joint occupation/sitting categories. Relative to agriculture, forestry or fishery work with sitting ≤7 hours/day, the adjusted MetS OR was 2.95 (95% CI 1.62–5.38) for office work with sitting ≤7 hours/day and 2.88 (1.61–5.13) for office work with sitting >7 hours/day. These are comparisons with a common external occupation/sitting reference, not a direct within-office estimate of additional sitting. The fitted sample of 4,303 adults included 1,831 nonworkers; worker-category contrasts are retained separately from an overall workforce estimate, and the occupational context of sitting cannot be isolated from total daily sitting. Survey-weighting and companion-sample questions remain open. [Nam et al., 2016, Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-016-3617-5).

### Shift work and component outcomes

In an Italian cross-sectional study of 272 healthcare workers, fasting glucose was reported as 94.7±5.4 mg/dL in 137 shift workers and 87.3±6.2 mg/dL in 135 nonshift workers (means±SD; unadjusted p<0.001). Shift workers had at least five years of shift exposure, and blood was collected at least three days after the last night shift. The sample excluded diabetes, systemic disease and medication use. This is an unadjusted fasting-glucose comparison; a separate regression treating employment condition as the outcome and HOMA-IR as the predictor does not establish that shift work caused MetS. [Ledda et al., 2019, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.3390/medicina55050186).

In a German population-based analysis, the primary current/recent night-shift comparison involved 69 participants per matched group without relevant medication. Reported means for PRESENT versus NEVER shift work were 121.41 versus 119.20 mmHg for systolic pressure, 73.05 versus 73.44 mmHg for diastolic pressure, 60.77 versus 57.35 mg/dL for HDL, 94.55 versus 104.89 mg/dL for triglycerides, and 91.74 versus 93.17 mg/dL for glucose. The respective reported MANCOVA group p-values were 0.201, 0.775, 0.137, 0.402 and 0.961. These comparisons do not demonstrate equivalence. The selected table lacks uncertainty measures for these means, PRESENT includes exposure within the preceding year, and current employment of never-shift comparators is not established. Former-worker comparisons and the larger medication-inclusive sensitivity sample are not substituted for this analysis. [Bittner et al., 2025, Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11357-025-01596-8).

## Discussion

The current work establishes a traceable provisional selection record and makes the remaining source and extraction gaps explicit. It does not yet establish the overall direction or magnitude of the relationship between workplace exposures and metabolic syndrome across the full retained evidence base. Those interpretations depend on completing the report-level findings, reconciling companion populations and evaluating their limitations.

Unavailable reports may alter the composition of the evidence available for synthesis. Their absence cannot be assumed to favour or oppose any particular finding. Records with uncertain eligibility and reports whose original results remain unverified will be identified separately so that readers can distinguish missing evidence from excluded evidence. The limitations will also state that the historical search record was reconstructed, supplementary search coverage remains under reconciliation, and independent review is incomplete.

Writing proceeds alongside extraction. Final exposure-specific conclusions, comparative strength-of-evidence statements and any quantitative synthesis will be developed only from verified eligible results. This permits progress on the manuscript while preserving the distinction between a working draft and a completed review.

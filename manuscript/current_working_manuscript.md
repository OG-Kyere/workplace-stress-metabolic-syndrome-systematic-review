# Workplace stress, dietary factors, physical activity and metabolic syndrome among working adults: a systematic review

Working manuscript, 17 October 2026. Selection counts are provisional. Study-level synthesis remains in development; the historical 34-report draft does not represent the current evidence set.

## Introduction

This review examines metabolic syndrome and its recognized components in employed adults. Its focus is the relationship between occupational stress, dietary factors, physical activity, sedentary behaviour and metabolic outcomes, together with workplace interventions addressing these exposures. The workplace context is central to eligibility: a general adult or clinical population is insufficient without an occupational population or a relevant worker-specific analysis.

The review distinguishes a diagnosis of metabolic syndrome from its individual components. It also distinguishes associations observed in occupational populations from effects estimated in intervention studies. These distinctions guide the extraction and synthesis, avoiding substitution of a broader cardiovascular marker for an eligible metabolic outcome or interpretation of a cross-sectional association as an intervention effect.

The objective is to synthesize eligible empirical evidence published from 2015 to the documented search cutoff in 2026, identifying the populations, exposures, outcomes and methodological limitations that determine how each finding can be interpreted.

## Methods

### Review history and eligibility

An archived undergraduate thesis provided the starting reference set. Its original screening spreadsheet was unavailable. The updated review therefore uses a reconstructed search and screening record rather than presenting the recovered records as the original historical database export. The archived thesis and historical study table are retained separately from the current evidence registers.

Eligibility requires an employed adult or explicitly occupational population; occupational stress, dietary factors, physical activity, sedentary behaviour or a relevant workplace lifestyle exposure/intervention; an eligible metabolic outcome; and completed original empirical research in an English-language peer-reviewed report within the publication window. The operational outcome rule accepts defined metabolic syndrome or recognized components explicitly related to metabolic-syndrome risk, prevention, severity or management. Components include central adiposity, blood pressure, fasting glycaemia, triglycerides and HDL cholesterol. BMI, fitness, stress scores or unrelated biomarkers alone do not establish outcome eligibility. Protocols, editorials, conference abstracts, secondary reviews and studies without an occupational context are excluded from the primary evidence synthesis.

Study design is determined from the reported methods. The broad criterion of completed original empirical research does not impose an undocumented categorical exclusion of every genetic design. Scope interpretations requiring confirmation are recorded explicitly in the screening evidence.

### Search and record management

The reconstructed PubMed/MEDLINE core query combines metabolic-syndrome terms with occupational population terms and stress, activity, sedentary, diet or nutrition terms, with publication limits covering 2015–2026. The executed query responses and downloaded records are preserved in the search archive. Additional source occurrences derive from documented supplementary queries, recovered references, review-reference mining, citation searching and version/companion discovery. A query's intended publication window is distinct from the date on which it was executed. No claim is made that publications appearing after a documented search execution were prospectively covered by that search.

Source occurrences are retained separately from canonical deduplicated records. Identifiers, bibliographic metadata and version relationships support reconciliation. Corrections, protocols and related publications are assessed according to their actual report type and contribution rather than assumed to be independent studies. Exact supplementary-search coverage and the final search cutoff require completion of the search-method reconciliation before the flow counts are frozen.

### Selection and unavailable reports

Selection proceeds through title/abstract screening and full-text assessment. Controlled exclusion reasons and the supporting evidence are recorded at the relevant stage. Late title/abstract exclusions remain distinguishable from full-text exclusions even when retrieval had already been attempted.

Existing retrieval attempts are retained. Further repeated retrieval attempts are deferred so that extraction and writing can proceed from available evidence. A report not retrieved is recorded separately from a retrieved report excluded for failing eligibility. A provisional record lacking archived original bytes is not automatically reclassified as a report never retrieved: previous web-based inspection, original-source verification and retention of a local copy are distinct states. Where eligibility cannot be established, it remains unresolved. A secondary review's account is not substituted for independently verified original numerical results.

Screening decisions and appraisal records currently constitute a Codex-assisted provisional first review. Independent reviewer verification has not been completed. This manuscript does not describe the process as independent duplicate screening.

### Data extraction and appraisal

Extraction records occupational population, design, recruitment and analysis denominators, exposure coding, comparator, metabolic outcome definition, follow-up, model adjustment, reported effect and uncertainty, and source location. Between-group intervention contrasts are distinguished from within-group change. Different adjustment models, outcome definitions, time points and participant strata remain separate. Null findings are retained; statistical significance is not an eligibility requirement.

Existing extraction fields are consolidated with their original file, row and hash provenance. Raw table cells support source review but do not by themselves constitute verified clinical effect estimates. Points outside their printed intervals and other internal source inconsistencies are retained as explicit quantitative holds; corrected values are not inferred. Lack of a numeric estimate in the current ledgers is not itself a reason to exclude an otherwise eligible report.

Design-specific JBI item assessments are recorded as Yes, No, Unclear or Not applicable where permitted. Current answers are provisional source-section assessments. They do not establish that full-report appraisal or independent checking is complete. Unclear answers remain visible rather than being replaced by assumed favourable judgments.

### Companion reports and synthesis

Reports are linked to provisional study-family identifiers when a common cohort or trial is identified. These identifiers do not yet certify complete participant independence. Population totals and pooled estimates will require reconciliation of recruitment periods, settings, eligibility, participant overlap and distinct outcomes across companion papers.

The synthesis will distinguish occupational stress, diet/nutrition, physical activity/sedentary behaviour and multicomponent workplace interventions. Work schedules and other related exposures will be described according to their operational eligibility classification. Observational associations will be separated from intervention effects, and syndrome-level outcomes from individual components. No pooled estimate or direction-of-effect conclusion is asserted in this working draft before study-level extraction and comparability assessment are complete.

## Results

### Selection: current provisional record

The current reconstruction contains 9,074 source occurrences. Removal of 3,725 duplicates leaves 5,349 canonical records screened, with 4,381 title/abstract exclusions and 968 reports sought for retrieval. Of those reports, 166 are recorded as not retrieved and 802 as available for full-text assessment. Available reports comprise 413 exclusions, 388 provisional inclusions and one report awaiting an age-eligibility decision. The 388 retained reports map to 372 provisional family identifiers; these are not presented as 372 confirmed independent cohorts or trials.

The current evidence, supplement and reference registers agree on the 388 provisional report identifiers. The older 34-report manuscript table and bibliography are historical artifacts and do not establish the current final study count. Final inclusion and flow totals remain subject to eligibility and search-method reconciliation.

### Availability and extraction

Among the 388 provisionally included reports, 343 have archived original article files, two have primary web-source review captures without verified original bytes, and 43 rely on review evidence pending original-source reconciliation. These 45 original-source gaps are distinct from the 166 reports recorded as not retrieved in the selection flow; the categories must not be added together or treated as interchangeable.

The 14 audited numerical/outcome ledgers contain 1,451 rows for 116 current reports, with exact source files and hashes verified for those rows. The remaining 272 reports have no rows in those particular ledgers, which does not establish absence of prior extraction elsewhere. Consolidation recovered prior structured fields for 71 of these 272 reports. Their next extraction actions involve 160 available articles, 67 PDF originals needing manual structured capture, and 45 original-source gaps.

The numerical audit identified 25 diagnostic flags: 17 point estimates outside printed intervals, seven CI/p-value compatibility warnings and one p-value outside the range zero to one. These are flags, not 25 independent erroneous studies. Model differences, rounding, table alignment and original reporting must be checked before a correction is accepted. Six missing carried-down exposure labels were restored in the processed audit from the original table, resolving two apparent conflicts between results for different exposures. Original numbers and historical ledgers were preserved.

### Appraisal and substantive findings

Provisional design-specific item answers cover all 388 retained reports, comprising 3,516 answers with 1,927 recorded as Unclear. Independent appraisal reviews completed: zero. These counts describe the current assessment record and do not support a conclusion that the evidence is generally high or low quality.

The following source-checked findings are an initial subset, not a synthesis of all 388 retained reports. The previous 34-report narrative is not carried forward as a conclusion about the expanded set. No pooled effect, aggregate participant total or claim that one exposure domain has the most consistent evidence is made at this stage. Four specified-results passes add 393 rows from 34 original articles. A whole-set reconciliation also recovered 40 existing rows from nine additional reports in a previously omitted extraction ledger. The expanded result index therefore covers 159 reports; 229 have no specified rows in those ledgers. Those 229 comprise 184 available originals still requiring detailed extraction and 45 original-source gaps whose further retrieval is deferred. A report with some extracted rows is not necessarily fully extracted or independently verified. Numerical-hold counts refer to rows rather than independent studies or newly established errors.

The previous 247-report gap list was checked as a complete set. Nine reports now have newly extracted table findings, nine have recovered existing rows, 45 have deferred original-source work, 37 retain prior source-linked narrative findings awaiting normalization, and 147 require further original-based extraction. These mutually exclusive categories total 247. A source-linked narrative or a verified file hash does not establish that the clinical result is fully extracted or correct.

### Workplace lifestyle education: between-group findings

In the Ethiopian bank-worker trial, 226 participants were allocated and 207 retained for analysis (105 intervention, 102 control). The reported adjusted GEE intervention-by-time coefficients were −1.501 cm for waist circumference (95% CI −4.052 to 1.049), −2.453 mmHg for systolic pressure (−6.289 to 1.382), and −0.620 mmHg for diastolic pressure (−3.181 to 1.941). All three intervals included zero. Triglyceride and fasting-glucose interaction coefficients also had intervals including zero: −16.281 mg/dL (−38.565 to 6.003) and −1.714 mg/dL (−9.417 to 5.990), respectively. HDL cholesterol decreased relative to the control time trend, with a coefficient of −5.055 mg/dL (−7.805 to −2.306); a decrease in HDL is not a favourable component change. These estimates differ from the larger reductions observed within the intervention arm. Allocation involved only two geographical clusters, one per arm, and recruitment followed allocation; individual-level GEE results do not resolve uncertainty about cluster-level treatment inference. Loss-to-follow-up exclusions also require reconciliation with the stated intention-to-treat analysis. [Bogale et al., 2025, Table 4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-22962-8).

### Physical activity and sedentary exposure

Among 344 employees of a Lebanese university, the original table reported MetS in 54 of 221 employees with low IPAQ activity and 27 of 123 with moderate/high activity. The unadjusted categorical comparison gave p=0.698. The paper therefore contributes an eligible activity comparison without clear evidence of an association in that analysis; its vitamin-D-status regression is not substituted for a dietary-intake or adjusted activity effect. [Ghadieh et al., 2018, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40795-018-0243-x).

In 373 currently employed Buffalo police officers, the mean number of MetS components across increasing physical-activity-intensity categories was 1.78, 1.57, 1.80 and 1.43, with respective SDs of 1.50, 1.37, 1.42 and 1.46 and a reported trend p=0.2112. This secondary, unadjusted comparison is distinct from the paper's main cortisol analysis. The protein-meal challenge was a physiological test and is not interpreted as a dietary-treatment effect. Activity-intensity units require clarification, and the BCOPS cohort relationship must be reconciled before combining this paper with companion reports. [Baughman et al., 2016, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajhb.22748).

A Korean survey analysis reported joint occupation/sitting categories. Relative to agriculture, forestry or fishery work with sitting ≤7 hours/day, the adjusted MetS OR was 2.95 (95% CI 1.62–5.38) for office work with sitting ≤7 hours/day and 2.88 (1.61–5.13) for office work with sitting >7 hours/day. These are comparisons with a common external occupation/sitting reference, not a direct within-office estimate of additional sitting. The fitted sample of 4,303 adults included 1,831 nonworkers; worker-category contrasts are retained separately from an overall workforce estimate, and the occupational context of sitting cannot be isolated from total daily sitting. Survey-weighting and companion-sample questions remain open. [Nam et al., 2016, Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-016-3617-5).

### Shift work and component outcomes

In an Italian cross-sectional study of 272 healthcare workers, fasting glucose was reported as 94.7±5.4 mg/dL in 137 shift workers and 87.3±6.2 mg/dL in 135 nonshift workers (means±SD; unadjusted p<0.001). Shift workers had at least five years of shift exposure, and blood was collected at least three days after the last night shift. The sample excluded diabetes, systemic disease and medication use. This is an unadjusted fasting-glucose comparison; a separate regression treating employment condition as the outcome and HOMA-IR as the predictor does not establish that shift work caused MetS. [Ledda et al., 2019, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.3390/medicina55050186).

In a German population-based analysis, the primary current/recent night-shift comparison involved 69 participants per matched group without relevant medication. Reported means for PRESENT versus NEVER shift work were 121.41 versus 119.20 mmHg for systolic pressure, 73.05 versus 73.44 mmHg for diastolic pressure, 60.77 versus 57.35 mg/dL for HDL, 94.55 versus 104.89 mg/dL for triglycerides, and 91.74 versus 93.17 mg/dL for glucose. The respective reported MANCOVA group p-values were 0.201, 0.775, 0.137, 0.402 and 0.961. These comparisons do not demonstrate equivalence. The selected table lacks uncertainty measures for these means, PRESENT includes exposure within the preceding year, and current employment of never-shift comparators is not established. Former-worker comparisons and the larger medication-inclusive sensitivity sample are not substituted for this analysis. [Bittner et al., 2025, Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11357-025-01596-8).

### Additional exercise and dietary comparisons

The Korean radiation-worker cohort reported exercise-frequency terms separately from its radiation analyses. Relative to no exercise, the reported Poisson RRs for incident MetS were 1.01 (95% CI 0.99–1.03) for exercise 1–6 times/week and 1.01 (0.94–1.06) for daily exercise. Lifestyle data were taken from the check-up closest to entry. Exercise-category totals and exact fitted-model details require reconciliation; radiation-dose effects and standardized incidence ratios are not treated as exercise estimates. [Noh et al., 2025, Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-025-23543-0).

In the Japanese worker prediction study, questionnaire responses at ages 30 and 35 were cross-tabulated by MetS status at age 40. Among men, exercise more than twice weekly was reported by 25.0% of later MetS cases and 16.9% of later noncases at age 30 (p=0.016), whereas the corresponding age-35 comparison gave p=0.918. These are exposure proportions conditional on later outcome status, not estimated incident risks. Breakfast reporting among men at age 35 also differed by later MetS status (p=0.006). These selected bivariate results neither establish protective exercise or dietary effects nor demonstrate absence of benefit. The age-specific datasets may share workers, item-level missingness varies and the participant-selection prose is contradictory. Prediction accuracy and feature importance are not substituted for exposure-effect estimates. [Suda et al., 2022, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1539/eohp.2021-0023-OA).

In 1,468 oil-company workers, the selected multivariable model reported an exercise OR of 0.672 (95% CI 0.499–0.905), with No/Yes coded 1/2. Moderate and salty preference categories had ORs of 2.362 (1.578–3.536) and 1.742 (1.146–2.648), respectively, against the table's light-salt category. Daily dairy intake had an OR of 0.317 (0.190–0.529). Dietary reference coding, omitted intermediate-category terms and complete-case model denominators require confirmation. These are cross-sectional, predictor-selected associations and do not estimate the effects of changing diet or exercise. [Wang et al., 2020, Tables 5–6](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-020-09921-w).

A Korean workplace-environment paper reported adjusted ORs of 0.86 (95% CI 0.59–1.25) for the table-labelled lack-of-activity category and 0.82 (0.58–1.16) for shift work. Both intervals include one. Reversals between published category totals and case/noncase columns, particularly for shift work, prevent confident interpretation of direction; these coefficients remain on a quantitative coding hold and do not support a protective-effect claim. [Jeong et al., 2018, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.15171/ijoem.2018.1346).

### Additional workplace intervention findings

The GSLiM trial's between-group waist-difference column reported −0.87 cm (95% CI −3.79 to 2.06) over weeks 0–12, −1.79 (−4.73 to 1.14) over weeks 13–24, −1.12 (−4.00 to 1.77) over weeks 0–24, and −1.44 (−4.39 to 1.52) in the subsequent follow-up phase. These intervals all include zero. The source table distinguishes mean differences, Cohen's d and p-values; they are not interchangeable. Repeated periods share participants. Lipid/glucose units printed as mmHg and a malformed diastolic-pressure confidence bound are preserved as unresolved source issues rather than corrected by assumption. [Jamal et al., 2016, Table 6](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0160343).

The nonrandomized Go! hospital programme reported a six-month waist percent-change contrast of 2.99% (95% CI 1.28–4.72), favouring the comparison group, and a twelve-month contrast of 0.54% (−1.06 to 2.16). These are contrasts from a log-transformed analysis, not centimetre differences. The hospital and clinic groups differed at baseline; imputation and covariate adjustment do not remove the limitations of the nonequivalent-group design. [LaCaille et al., 2016, Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-016-2828-0).

The randomized worksite-wellness study reported programme-availability differences of 0.2 mmHg (95% CI −1.7 to 2.2) for systolic pressure, 0.5 mmHg (−0.8 to 1.8) for diastolic pressure, −0.3 mg/dL (−2.4 to 1.7) for HDL cholesterol and −1.1 percentage points (−5.8 to 3.6) for low HDL. None showed a clear clinical benefit in these selected comparisons. The administrative population of 32,974 employees is not the biometric analysis sample. Programme availability is also distinct from the separately reported local effect of programme participation. Outcome-specific denominators, screening participation and the paper's correction notice remain relevant to interpretation. [Song and Baicker, 2019, clinical-outcomes table](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2019.3307).

Among semiconductor workers who selected diet-plus-exercise or exercise-only programmes, the weight-change-adjusted model reported coefficients of −0.46 mmHg (95% CI −2.74 to 1.81) for systolic pressure, −0.18 mmHg (−1.78 to 1.42) for diastolic pressure and 0.23 (−12.06 to 12.53, source triglyceride scale) for triglyceride change. All three intervals include zero. Change was defined as pre-test minus post-test, and the weight-change-adjusted coefficient addresses a different estimand from the intervention-group model without that adjustment. Self-selection, shared factories and the absence of an untreated control limit causal interpretation. [Lin et al., 2018, Table 4](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bj.2018.09.002).

The meat-processing walking feasibility trial reports waist and blood-pressure changes primarily within each arm. For example, the intervention-arm twelve-week waist change is printed as −1.7 cm (95% CI −3.8 to 0.2) with p=0.029, an unresolved CI/p-value inconsistency. The intervention-arm twenty-four-week systolic-pressure change is −4.1 mmHg (−8.6 to 0.2) with p=0.030. Neither is substituted for a between-arm treatment effect. Five missing participants were replaced with group means, and no corrected confidence bounds or inferred treatment-by-time coefficients are supplied here. [Mansi et al., 2015, Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-015-1736-z).

In the Danish SoSu-life trial, the main completer analysis reported an adjusted thirty-eight-week waist difference of −1.79 cm (95% CI −3.09 to −0.49; 129 intervention and 116 control observations for that endpoint). At sixteen weeks, the corresponding estimate was −1.05 cm (−2.26 to 0.16). Main-group blood-pressure intervals included zero at both assessments. Waist analyses excluded smoking-pledge participants, and the weight-loss-pledge subgroup is nested within the intervention arm, not a separate trial. Attrition, voluntary pledges and the stated municipality-level random effect require consideration when interpreting the reported estimates. [Balk-Møller et al., 2017, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.6196).

The peer-modelling pilot provides descriptive pre/post component means for 23 attention-control and 17 intervention completers. HDL decreased from 60.4 to 57.8 mg/dL in intervention completers and from 54.2 to 53.9 mg/dL in controls. Triglycerides increased from 99.1 to 105.7 mg/dL and from 125.3 to 163.7 mg/dL, respectively. These descriptive changes are not adjusted treatment contrasts and do not establish a universal lipid benefit. No paired uncertainty or between-group estimate is manufactured from the reported marginal means and SDs. [Rowland et al., 2018, outcomes table](https://doi.org/10.1177/2165079917753690).

### Dietary behaviour, education and component outcomes

Among 1,215 healthcare workers, the eating-disorder screening group had 17 MetS cases among 63 workers (27.0% as printed), compared with 172 cases among the other workers (14.9% as printed). Hypertension, low HDL, hypertriglyceridaemia and hyperglycaemia were also described by eating-disorder group. These are unadjusted cross-sectional comparisons; the source's regression for violence and stress predicts eating-disorder symptoms, not MetS. It cannot be inverted into a violence-to-MetS effect. Endpoint-specific denominators and the printed HDL percentage need confirmation. [Magnavita et al., 2025, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph22081221).

The Spanish Mediterranean-diet programme followed 7,034 selected workers with initially low adherence, including 4,038 men and 2,996 women. Reported mean waist circumference changed from 100.8 to 98.3 cm in men and from 104.3 to 98.7 cm in women. NCEP-ATP III MetS prevalence changed from 27.5% to 22.5% and from 23.7% to 20.5%, respectively; IDF-defined prevalence was reported separately for the same participants. The study also reports pre/post blood-pressure, glycaemic and lipid comparisons. These observations lack a concurrent control, and the different syndrome definitions do not create independent samples. Neither marginal SDs nor rounded prevalence values provide a paired-change variance or exact case count. [Ramírez Gallegos et al., 2024, Tables 2–4](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu16071082).

In the Italian sodium-education programme, 95 clinical-phase completers had median systolic pressure of 130 mmHg (IQR 120–140) at baseline and 120 (120–130) at follow-up, and median diastolic pressure of 85 (80–92) and 80 (75–80), respectively. Median abdominal circumference was 90 cm (78–98) and 89 (78–98). These were paired, uncontrolled observations after voluntary two-stage recruitment from a workforce of 4,911. Differences between the marginal medians are not reported as medians of individual change, and the results do not isolate an intervention effect from selection or regression to the mean. [Spiteri et al., 2023, Table 5](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph20085478).

The Jigjiga public-sector paper reports adjusted associations of activity, dietary behaviours and work stress with central obesity. Its no-exercise coefficient is printed as OR 1.84 (95% CI 1.03–3.30), and the No-versus-Yes work-stress coefficient as 0.44 (0.20–0.96). These remain on a source-denominator hold: the displayed exercise categories contain 69 and 614 workers, totalling 683 despite a stated sample of 623. Dietary estimates from that model are preserved in the extraction record but are not promoted to reliable quantitative findings until the discrepancy is resolved. The stress reference direction is retained without inversion. [Hussein et al., 2025, Table 4](https://doi.org/10.1177/22799036251388562).

### Further shift-work and mobile-programme findings

Among Korean male chemical-plant workers, unadjusted means±SD were 129.97±9.22 versus 128.95±8.59 mmHg for systolic pressure and 81.03±7.51 versus 79.69±7.36 mmHg for diastolic pressure in 1,932 shift and 1,094 day workers. Triglycerides were 143.34±92.39 versus 135.30±109.77 mg/dL. The reported p-values were 0.003, <0.001 and 0.032, respectively. Fasting-glucose and HDL comparisons gave p=0.967 and p=0.435. These component results are unadjusted; the paper's adjusted TyG-index OR is a different endpoint and is not substituted for an adjusted component effect. [Baek et al., 2024, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.35371/aoem.2024.36.e18).

The Italian shift-working healthcare-worker survey reports hypertension percentages across years of shift work. Men's percentages were 6.7%, 12.1%, 29.4% and 56.2%, and women's 5.5%, 8.1%, 19.8% and 25.1% across the <10, 10–20, 21–30 and 31–40-year categories. These descriptive percentages lack a day-worker comparator and are potentially confounded by age. The table's significance markers compare sexes, not duration trends. The adjusted cardiovascular-risk-category models do not supply adjusted hypertension effects. [d'Ettorre et al., 2026, Table 5](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm15114028).

The Iranian metal-manufacturing report compares 204 shift and 204 day workers. Its table distinguishes summaries labelled “2022” and “10-Years”; these are retained as labelled rather than recast as baseline and final visits. The 2022 fasting-glucose means were 92.36±16.54 versus 84.65±10.98 mg/dL, while systolic-pressure means were 123±9 versus 121±8 mmHg. The selected comparison p-values were 0.037 and 0.128. Separate within-group repeated-measures trend tests do not establish a group-by-time difference. Survivor selection, baseline matching and the interpretation of the ten-year summaries remain important limitations. [Soltanzadeh et al., 2024, Tables 4–5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-68378-8).

The Korean mobile chronic-disease programme provides propensity-matched hypertension, diabetes and obesity cohorts that may overlap. In the hypertension cohort (30 participants and 60 controls), systolic- and diastolic-pressure group-by-time p-values were 0.41 and 0.13; a follow-up diastolic group p=0.045 is not the intervention-change test. In the obesity cohort (44 participants and 88 controls), triglyceride and HDL group-by-time tests were both reported as p<0.001. The programme lasted twelve weeks in 2021, whereas health-examination outcomes were compared between 2019 and 2023; this is not four years of continuous treatment. Estimated marginal means use SEs, not SDs, and neither matching nor adjustment for residual imbalance establishes random allocation or removes unmeasured confounding. [Park et al., 2026, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.2196/82822).

### Further source-checked stress, dietary and activity findings

In a Spanish workforce analysis of 23,729 participants, acceptable Mediterranean-diet adherence was associated with a reported MetS OR of 0.76 (95% CI 0.67–0.86) and central-obesity OR of 0.64 (0.56–0.73); the hypertension OR was 0.89 (0.78–1.02). The source table uses different adjustment sets for central obesity and for MetS/hypertension, with the latter including adiposity variables. Cross-sectional associations, forward-Wald variable selection and the overlapping overall/sex-specific analyses limit interpretation as dietary effects. [Ramón-Arbués et al., 2020, Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1590/1518-8345.3904.3295).

The Italian police follow-up paper's conditional stress coefficients were 0.921 (95% CI 0.510–1.661), 0.830 (0.446–1.547) and 0.746 (0.381–1.463) in successive sleep-adjusted models. In the final model, the insomnia coefficient was 11.038 (2.867–42.493). These are different exposures and conditional estimands; the insomnia estimate is not a direct work-stress effect, and sleep adjustment may condition on variables in the proposed pathway. Averaged sleep measures span the outcome interval, only 26 incident cases were reported, and sample/risk-set denominators require reconciliation. Possible overlap with other police-cohort reports remains to be assessed. [Garbarino et al., 2019, Tables 3–4](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0224259).

The Bahia nursing paper reports adjusted prevalence ratios of 1.25 (95% CI 1.02–1.53) for its activity term and 1.45 (1.17–1.81) for burnout. The Methods describe backward logistic selection followed by robust Poisson/OR-to-PR conversion terminology; exact estimation and reference coding remain flagged. The same Bahia nursing population may contribute to another retained report, so the findings do not establish an independent additional sample. In a separate study of 168 female nurses, emotional-exhaustion tertile 2 versus tertile 1 had an adjusted central-obesity OR of 14.95 (1.50–148.71), illustrating the imprecision of selected component results. A component association is not a full-syndrome association. Original Methods and Table 2 both use personal-accomplishment tertile 3 as reference; the prior note suggesting a reference conflict was withdrawn after source inspection. [das Merces et al., 2019, Table 3](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph16152686); [Chico-Barba et al., 2019, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph16111993).

Among 430 initially MetS-free workers in the Ryobi cohort, Model 3 HRs for prolonged sedentary bouts of at least 30 minutes were 2.72 (95% CI 1.30–5.73), 2.42 (1.11–5.25) and 2.85 (1.31–6.18) for quartiles 2–4 versus quartile 1. Total sedentary-time and non-prolonged-time contrasts were also extracted and their corresponding intervals included one in Model 3. Models additionally conditioning on waist circumference are retained separately. The table footnote includes family income in Model 2 whereas the Methods list omits it; this reporting difference is preserved. Models and sedentary domains use the same participants and 83 events. [Honda et al., 2016, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-016-3570-3).

In a cross-sectional police study, leisure-time activity was reported as 18.32 (SD 25.84) versus 29.67 (27.15) corrected MET-minutes/week/kg in participants with and without MetS (p=0.0001); total activity was 168.32 (117.62) versus 192.60 (151.84), p=0.22. The unusual corrected units are retained. These descriptive comparisons do not make the paper's separate plaque regressions into adjusted MetS/activity models. Among 902 fixed-shift poultry workers, >3 versus ≤3 meals/day had a crude MetS PR of 0.49 (0.29–0.83) and adjusted PR of 0.43 (0.26–0.73), from a hierarchical robust-Poisson analysis. The comparison is cross-sectional and does not estimate incident risk or the effect of assigning more meals. [Janczura et al., 2015, Table 6](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0133750); [Canuto et al., 2015, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1590/s0034-8910.2015049005524).

In the vitamin-D trial among rotating mine workers, four-month waist-change medians were −0.1 cm (IQR −1.8 to 1.4) for placebo, 0.37 (−1.25 to 1.6) for 14,000 IU/week and 0.8 (−1 to 1.83) for 28,000 IU/week. All three waist groups share the source's nonsignificant post-hoc letter. The ranges are IQRs, not confidence intervals. The 198 participants are the per-protocol analysis population following 120 post-randomization exclusions, not the number originally randomized. The eight-week REHIT versus MICT trial analyzed 27 of 32 randomized participants. REHIT waist means changed from 86.6 to 85.4 cm and systolic pressure from 130.4 to 123.8 mmHg; corresponding MICT means were 87.6 to 87.3 cm and 128.9 to 126.7 mmHg. Diastolic pressure and glucose do not carry that treatment-change marker. The extracted arm means and SDs are not converted into paired effect uncertainties, and the small completer analysis limits inference. [Cuddy et al., 2019, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph16030483).

## Discussion

The current work establishes a traceable provisional selection record and makes the remaining source and extraction gaps explicit. It does not yet establish the overall direction or magnitude of the relationship between workplace exposures and metabolic syndrome across the full retained evidence base. Those interpretations depend on completing the report-level findings, reconciling companion populations and evaluating their limitations.

Unavailable reports may alter the composition of the evidence available for synthesis. Their absence cannot be assumed to favour or oppose any particular finding. Records with uncertain eligibility and reports whose original results remain unverified will be identified separately so that readers can distinguish missing evidence from excluded evidence. The limitations will also state that the historical search record was reconstructed, supplementary search coverage remains under reconciliation, and independent review is incomplete.

Writing proceeds alongside extraction. Final exposure-specific conclusions, comparative strength-of-evidence statements and any quantitative synthesis will be developed only from verified eligible results. This permits progress on the manuscript while preserving the distinction between a working draft and a completed review.

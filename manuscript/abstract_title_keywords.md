# Title

Workplace Stress, Diet, Physical Activity and Metabolic Syndrome Among Working Adults: A Systematic Review

# Structured Abstract

## Background

Metabolic syndrome (MetS) is influenced by behavioural and occupational factors that may cluster within working populations. Workplace stress, dietary patterns, physical inactivity, and sedentary behaviour have each been linked with metabolic risk, while workplaces also provide a practical setting for preventive interventions.

## Aims

To synthesize evidence on associations between workplace stress, dietary factors, physical activity, sedentary behaviour, and MetS among working adults, and to evaluate workplace-based lifestyle interventions targeting MetS or its established components.

## Methods

A workplace-focused systematic review of peer-reviewed studies published from 2015 through 2026 was undertaken. Eligible studies included employed adults or occupational populations and evaluated workplace stress, diet, physical activity, sedentary behaviour, or multicomponent workplace lifestyle interventions in relation to MetS or recognized MetS components. Randomized controlled trials, prospective cohort studies, analytical cross-sectional studies, and quasi-experimental interventions were eligible. Study quality was assessed using design-specific Joanna Briggs Institute critical appraisal tools. Owing to substantial heterogeneity in populations, exposures, interventions, MetS definitions, and effect measures, findings were synthesized narratively.

## Results

Across occupational settings, adverse work stress was generally associated with less favourable metabolic profiles, with prospective cohorts providing the strongest temporal evidence. Higher leisure-time physical activity was associated with lower MetS incidence or prevalence, whereas prolonged sedentary exposure was associated with greater MetS risk. Healthier dietary patterns were generally associated with more favourable metabolic outcomes, and workplace dietary interventions improved several MetS components. Randomized and quasi-experimental workplace programmes combining physical activity, nutrition, counselling, or digital support produced improvements in waist circumference, blood pressure, glucose regulation, lipid profiles, and MetS severity in several studies. The evidence base was heterogeneous, and many observational studies were cross-sectional.

## Conclusions

Workplace stress, diet, physical activity, and sedentary behaviour appear to be important modifiable correlates of MetS among working adults. Workplace-based lifestyle interventions can improve several metabolic outcomes, although confidence is greatest for randomized and prospective evidence. More longitudinal studies and rigorously designed workplace trials are needed across diverse occupational populations.

# Cover Letter — Occupational Medicine

Dear Editor,

Please consider the manuscript **“Workplace Determinants of Metabolic Syndrome in Working Adults: Systematic Review”** for publication as a Systematic Review in *Occupational Medicine*.

Metabolic syndrome is an important occupational-health concern because several of its modifiable determinants—including psychosocial work stress, dietary behaviour, physical inactivity and sedentary work—are shaped by the working environment. At the same time, workplaces provide a practical setting for preventive interventions.

This review synthesizes evidence from working and occupational populations across four related domains: occupational stress, diet and workplace nutrition, physical activity and sedentary behaviour, and multicomponent workplace lifestyle interventions. The review deliberately distinguishes cross-sectional associations from prospective and randomized evidence and uses design-specific Joanna Briggs Institute appraisal tools to qualify confidence in the findings.

The findings indicate that adverse occupational stress is associated with greater metabolic risk, while healthier dietary patterns, greater physical activity and lower sedentary exposure are generally associated with more favourable metabolic profiles. Workplace-based lifestyle interventions also improve several metabolic-syndrome components, although the strength of inference varies by study design.

We believe the manuscript is well suited to *Occupational Medicine* because it focuses specifically on employed populations and translates evidence into implications for workplace prevention, screening and health promotion.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has not been published previously in this form. No external funding was received, and the author declares no competing interests.

All eligibility decisions, methodological judgments, extracted results, interpretations, and final manuscript content were reviewed by the author against the underlying sources.

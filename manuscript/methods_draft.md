# Draft Methods Section

## Design and reporting framework

This systematic review was updated and restructured to evaluate the relationships between workplace stress, dietary factors, physical activity, and metabolic syndrome (MetS) among working adults. The review was conducted using a workplace-focused eligibility framework and reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) principles.

The original undergraduate thesis served as the historical starting point for the review. Because the original screening spreadsheet was no longer available and the archived thesis study set was found to contain eligibility inconsistencies, a fresh literature search and re-screening process were undertaken for the revised manuscript.

## Eligibility criteria

Studies were eligible if they met all of the following criteria:

### Population and context
Participants were adults in employment, occupational groups, or explicitly defined workplace settings.

### Exposures and interventions
Eligible exposures or interventions included:
- occupational or workplace stress;
- job strain or related psychosocial work factors;
- dietary patterns, diet quality, nutrition, or workplace food interventions;
- physical activity, exercise, sedentary behaviour, or work-related activity;
- multicomponent workplace lifestyle interventions incorporating one or more of these factors.

### Outcomes
Studies were required to report either:
1. MetS as a defined syndrome, including prevalence, incidence, severity, diagnosis, remission, or risk; or
2. recognized MetS components explicitly framed in relation to MetS prevention, risk, severity, or management.

Recognized components included central or abdominal obesity, elevated blood pressure, impaired fasting glucose or hyperglycaemia, elevated triglycerides, and reduced HDL cholesterol.

Studies reporting only general stress, work ability, broad wellness measures, or non-MetS biomarkers without a clear metabolic-syndrome framework were excluded.

### Study designs
Completed original empirical studies were eligible, including randomized controlled trials, prospective or longitudinal cohort studies, analytical cross-sectional studies, and quasi-experimental workplace interventions.

Study protocols, editorials, conference abstracts, grey literature, and secondary reviews were excluded from the primary evidence synthesis.

### Publication limits
English-language peer-reviewed studies published from 2015 through 2026 were eligible.

## Search strategy

A fresh literature search was undertaken because the original thesis screening file was unavailable and the archived study list was not sufficiently consistent with the workplace-focused review question.

The primary search concepts combined terms for:
1. metabolic syndrome;
2. workers, employees, workplaces, occupations, and occupational groups; and
3. workplace stress, job strain, physical activity, sedentary behaviour, diet, nutrition, and workplace lifestyle interventions.

A core PubMed/MEDLINE search concept was structured as:

```text
("metabolic syndrome"[Title/Abstract] OR "Metabolic Syndrome"[MeSH Terms])
AND
(worker*[Title/Abstract] OR employee*[Title/Abstract] OR workplace[Title/Abstract]
 OR occupation*[Title/Abstract] OR "shift work"[Title/Abstract]
 OR "white-collar"[Title/Abstract] OR "blue-collar"[Title/Abstract]
 OR police[Title/Abstract] OR driver*[Title/Abstract]
 OR healthcare[Title/Abstract])
AND
(stress[Title/Abstract] OR "job strain"[Title/Abstract]
 OR "physical activity"[Title/Abstract] OR exercise[Title/Abstract]
 OR sedentary[Title/Abstract] OR diet*[Title/Abstract]
 OR nutrition*[Title/Abstract])
AND
("2015"[Date - Publication] : "2026"[Date - Publication])
```

Supplementary searches were conducted using narrower exposure-specific combinations for occupational stress, physical activity, sedentary work, diet, nutrition, shift work, and workplace interventions.

Additional studies were identified through reference-list screening, citation chasing, publisher records, PubMed-indexed related articles, and review-article reference mining.

The final database-specific search strings, exact search dates, and record counts will be archived in the supplementary search log when the formal search is closed.

## Study selection

Screening was performed in two stages:
1. title and abstract screening;
2. full-text eligibility assessment.

Studies were assessed against the predefined workplace population, exposure/intervention, outcome, study-design, and publication criteria.

Where a publication represented a protocol for a later completed study, only the completed study was retained in the effectiveness synthesis.

Where multiple reports arose from the same underlying cohort or intervention programme, the reports were linked and treated as companion publications rather than independent populations when participant totals or evidence weight were summarized.

## Data extraction

A structured extraction table was developed to capture:
- author and publication year;
- country and occupational population;
- study design;
- sample size and follow-up duration;
- exposure or intervention;
- MetS definition or metabolic outcome;
- effect estimates and confidence intervals where reported;
- principal findings;
- methodological limitations;
- DOI or bibliographic identifier.

Effect estimates were preferentially extracted from adjusted analyses when available.

For intervention studies, between-group effects were prioritized over within-group changes where reported.

## Study-design classification

Included studies were classified as:
- randomized controlled trials;
- prospective or longitudinal cohort studies;
- analytical cross-sectional studies;
- quasi-experimental or nonrandomized workplace interventions.

Design classification was based on the study Methods rather than article title alone.

## Critical appraisal

Methodological quality was assessed using design-specific Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) critical appraisal tools.

The following tools were assigned:
- JBI Critical Appraisal Tool for Randomized Controlled Trials;
- JBI Critical Appraisal Tool for Cohort Studies;
- JBI Assessment of Risk of Bias for Analytical Cross-sectional Studies;
- JBI Critical Appraisal Tool for Quasi-Experimental Studies.

Items were judged as Yes, No, Unclear, or Not applicable where permitted.

Studies were not excluded solely on the basis of a numerical appraisal score. Instead, appraisal findings were used to judge the credibility and relative weight of evidence during synthesis.

## Handling of overlapping cohorts and companion reports

Potentially overlapping reports were reviewed to avoid double-counting participants.

For example, the 2020 and 2021 Dutch Lifelines workforce publications were retained because they addressed different analytical questions, but they were treated as reports from the same underlying cohort when population totals and study independence were considered.

Similarly, protocol or feasibility papers linked to completed workplace intervention trials were treated as companion reports unless they contributed unique prespecified outcomes relevant to the review.

## Data synthesis

Because the studies varied substantially in occupational populations, exposure definitions, intervention content, MetS definitions, study designs, and reported effect measures, the primary synthesis was narrative.

Evidence was organized into four domains:
1. occupational and work-related stress;
2. diet and workplace nutrition;
3. physical activity and sedentary behaviour;
4. multicomponent workplace lifestyle interventions.

Interpretation prioritized evidence hierarchically:
1. randomized controlled trials;
2. prospective cohort studies;
3. quasi-experimental studies;
4. analytical cross-sectional studies.

Cross-sectional findings were interpreted as associations and were not described using causal language.

A quantitative meta-analysis will only be considered for outcome subsets that demonstrate sufficient comparability in population, exposure/intervention, MetS definition, and effect measure.
